One generation, up close

Select, mix, tweak. The three moves that repeat 500 times, and the three settings that control them.

A gardener saving seeds

A gardener who wants sweeter tomatoes keeps some of this year's plants, crosses the best ones to get seedlings, and accepts that a few seedlings will turn out odd. Next year they do it again. One round of that is a generation, and the whole crowd of 100 routes is the population.

30 kept
70 children (35 pairs of parents, 2 children each)

That split comes from one setting, the replacement rate, which defaults to 0.7. This is the arithmetic in ga.py.

CODE 
    num_pairs = round(r * p / 2)
    num_children = 2 * num_pairs
    keep_n = p - num_children

    assert keep_n >= 0

    return keep_n, num_pairs
PLAIN ENGLISH

How many pairs of parents do we need? Take the replacement share of the population and halve it. With 0.7 and 100 that is 35.

Each pair makes two children, so 70 children.

Whatever is left over is carried into the next generation: 30.

Safety check: that leftover number can never be negative.

Hand both numbers back to whoever asked.

Move 1: pick a parent with a mini-contest

To choose a parent, the program draws five routes at random and takes the shortest of the five. This is called tournament selection.

CODE 
    # Pick k random indices
    indices = [rng.randrange(len(costs)) for _ in range(k)]
    
    # Return the index with the lowest cost
    return min(indices, key=lambda i: costs[i])
PLAIN ENGLISH

A note: choose k contestants.

Draw k random position numbers from the population. With the default settings, k is 5.

A note: the shortest route wins.

Of those contestants, hand back the position of the one whose route is shortest.

💡
Selection pressure

The contest size is a dial. A bigger contest means the very best routes win almost every time, so the crowd improves fast but soon all looks alike. A smaller contest gives weaker routes a chance and keeps variety alive. Engineers call this dial "selection pressure".

Move 2: mix two routes without breaking them

A route must contain every city exactly once. Gluing half of one route to half of another would repeat some cities and drop others. The mixing method here, order crossover, avoids that.

Parent A 0 1 2 3 4 5 6 7
Parent B 3 7 0 5 1 6 2 4
Child 5 1 2 3 4 6 7 0

The teal stretch (2, 3, 4) is copied straight from Parent A. Parent B then supplies the rest in its own order, starting after the stretch and wrapping around: 6, 7, 0, 5, 1. Cities already used are skipped.

CODE 
    child = [None] * n
    child[cut1:cut2+1] = p1[cut1:cut2+1]
    
    used= set(child[cut1:cut2+1])
    
    fill=[]
    for city in p2[cut2+1:] + p2[:cut2+1]:
        if city not in used:
            fill.append(city)
PLAIN ENGLISH

Start the child as a row of empty slots, one per city.

Copy the stretch between the two cut points from the first parent into the same slots.

Remember which cities are now taken.

Start an empty waiting list.

Walk through the second parent, beginning just after the stretch and wrapping around to the front.

If a city is not already taken…

…add it to the waiting list. The list then fills the empty slots in order.

Move 3: a small random tweak

Mixing only reshuffles what the crowd already has. A mutation introduces something new. The project has two kinds, and a setting chooses between them.

🔀

Swap

Two cities trade places.
0 1 2 3 4 5 6 7
0 1 5 3 4 2 6 7
Up to four roads in the route change.

↩️

Inversion

A stretch is driven in reverse.
0 1 2 3 4 5 6 7
0 1 5 4 3 2 6 7
Only two roads change, so it is gentler.

CODE 
    # Copy the tour
    new_tour = tour.copy()
    
    # Pick two random positions
    i, j = sorted(rng.sample(range(len(tour)), 2))
    
    # Reverse the part between them
    new_tour[i:j+1] = reversed(new_tour[i:j+1])

    # Return the copy
    return new_tour
PLAIN ENGLISH

Work on a copy, so the original route is left untouched.

Choose two different positions at random and put the smaller one first.

Flip the order of everything between those two positions.

Hand back the changed copy.

📝
Why the default is inversion

The settings table in experiments.py chooses inversion, and a note beside it records why: on berlin52 with random starting routes, inversion finished about 8.4% from the best possible route against about 24.8% for swap.

The three moves, assembled

This is the heart of ga.py, the part that runs once per generation. A loop appears three times: once to keep, once to breed, once to tweak.

CODE 
        # Survivors: keep_n = (1-r)p picked by tournament selection
        new_population = []
        for _ in range(keep_n):
            i = tournament_selection(costs, k, rng)
            new_population.append(population[i][:])

            child2 = order_crossover(population[j], population[i], rng)

            new_population.append(child1)
            new_population.append(child2)

        for i in positions:

PLAIN ENGLISH

Start next year's crowd as an empty list.

30 times: hold a mini-contest and carry a copy of the winner forward. The same strong route can win more than once.

35 times: hold two mini-contests to find two parents.

Mix them twice, swapping which parent donates the copied stretch, to get two different children.

Add both children to next year's crowd.

Choose 10 positions at random out of the 100. That is the mutation rate of 0.1.

Replace each chosen route with a tweaked copy of itself. Any route can be chosen, including the best one.

Three dials to steer with

k = 5 Contest size. Higher favours the best routes more strongly.
r = 0.7 Replacement rate. Higher means more children and fewer routes carried over.
m = 0.1 Mutation rate. Higher means more random tweaks per generation.
